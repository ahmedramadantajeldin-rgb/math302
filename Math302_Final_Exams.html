Question 1

In solving by the Jacobi scheme using the recurrence $x^{(n+1)} = J x^{(n)} + h$ the equations:

$100x_1 - x_2 - x_3 - x_4 = -3$
$-x_1 - x_2 + 100x_3 - x_4 = 199$
$-x_1 + 100x_2 - x_3 - x_4 = 98$
$-x_1 - x_2 - x_3 + 100x_4 = -3$

on a 10-digit machine, we obtained — starting from a zero initial guess for all the unknowns — the following result after performing just one iteration:

$x_1^{(1)} = -0.03$, $x_2^{(1)} = 0.98$, $x_3^{(1)} = 1.99$, $x_4^{(1)} = -0.03$

(i) Perform one further iteration and calculate its accuracy.

(ii) If the accuracy is governed by the inequality $\|x^{(n)} - x\| \le \frac{\|J\|^n}{1 - \|J\|} \|x^{(1)} - x^{(0)}\|$ where $x$ is the true solution, then what is the number of iterations needed to reach at least 9 correct decimal digits; i.e. less than $10^{-9}$.

Solution

$x_1 = -0.03 + 0.01x_2 + 0.01x_3 + 0.01x_4$
$x_2 = 0.98 + 0.01x_1 + 0.01x_3 + 0.01x_4$
$x_3 = 1.99 + 0.01x_1 + 0.01x_2 + 0.01x_4$
$x_4 = -0.03 + 0.01x_1 + 0.01x_2 + 0.01x_3$

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0.01 & 0.01 & 0.01 \\ 0.01 & 0 & 0.01 & 0.01 \\ 0.01 & 0.01 & 0 & 0.01 \\ 0.01 & 0.01 & 0.01 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \xrightarrow{\text{row sums}} 0.03$$

$\|J\| = 0.03 < 1$, the method is quickly convergent.

The method is convergent.
